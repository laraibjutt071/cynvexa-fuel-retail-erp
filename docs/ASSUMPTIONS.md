# Open Owner/Accountant Decisions & Implemented Defaults

The blueprint (`docs/blueprint/...docx`) is a discovery document, not an approved spec. It explicitly marks a number of business rules as unresolved. Per the engineering brief, each one below is implemented as a **safe, configurable default** so development is not blocked, not as a silently invented permanent rule. Nothing here should be read as a legal, tax, or accounting determination.

Status values: `OPEN` (default implemented, needs owner/accountant sign-off) · `CONFIRMED` (owner/accountant has signed off — update this file when that happens, with date and who confirmed it).

| 17 | §12 (Phase C) | Who approves a flagged meter replacement/reset/rollover event | **Implemented (Phase C):** the same permission as shift approval (`shift:approve`) gates `PATCH .../nozzle-readings/{id}/approve-meter-event`, and a shift cannot itself be approved while any flagged meter event is still unapproved. | 18 | — (not in blueprint) | Whether a closed or approved shift can ever be reopened/corrected | **Partially implemented (Phase D).** The *financial* record of a shift now has a real correction mechanism: `ShiftTender`/`ShiftCashMovement` support reversal (even after shift approval), and `ShiftReconciliation` supports approved amendments to `actual_physical_cash`/`explanation` - see #23. | 19 | — (not in blueprint) | What currency this system operates in | **Not implemented beyond a label.** `Organization.currency_code` is a free-text, display-only field (no validation against ISO 4217, no effect on any calculation). This was never actually asked for before Phase D introduced the first money values - now that it has, the gap is worth the owner/accountant knowing about explicitly. | Owner + accountant | OPEN |
| 20 | — (not in blueprint) | Money rounding convention | **Implemented (Phase D), a specific default chosen, not an accounting determination.** `app/core/revenue.py::round_money` rounds half-up to 2 decimal places, applied ONCE to a shift's total expected revenue (after summing every unrounded litre-segment × price line item), never per line item before summing. An accountant may require round-half-even ("banker's rounding"), rounding at a different number of decimal places, or per-line rounding instead - any of those would change the exact cent-level figures this system reports. | Accountant | OPEN |
| 21 | §7 (adapted to cash) | How "material" is defined for a shift's financial discrepancy | **Implemented (Phase D).** Two independent tolerances, either breach makes a reconciliation `requires_authorization`: `Station.cash_variance_tolerance_amount` (an absolute money amount, default 50.00 - chosen because a fixed miscount threshold is the real-world control point, not a percentage of a number that varies wildly shift to shift) for the physical-cash shortage/excess, and `Station.revenue_variance_tolerance_percent` (default 1.0%) for expected-revenue-vs-tender. Both defaults, and the choice to use "either breach" rather than "both must breach," are this implementation's own reasonable defaults. | Owner + accountant | OPEN |
| 22 | §8 (adapted) | Credit customer master data | **Deliberately minimal (Phase D).** `CreditCustomer` is just an id/name/is_active stub, pulled forward from Phase E only so a `ShiftTender` of category "credit" has something to reference for the receivable. Credit limits, running balance, ageing, statements and collections - everything blueprint §8 actually describes for credit customers - remain unbuilt; this is not the Phase E feature, just enough to not block Phase D. | Owner | OPEN — deferred |
| 23 | §12 (Phase D) | Who is allowed to *record* reconciliation inputs (tenders, cash movements, the reconciliation itself) versus who *approves/authorizes* them | **Implemented (Phase D), deliberately the strictest reading of §12 so far.** `shift_reconcile:record` goes to Cashier/Operator, Station Manager, Shift Supervisor, Accountant and Owner (recording is low-risk - it's a factual entry). `shift_reconcile:approve` (approving a cash movement, reversing a tender/movement, authorizing a material discrepancy, approving a reconciliation amendment) goes ONLY to Accountant and Owner - Station Manager and Shift Supervisor do NOT get it, even though they get the equivalent approval permission for fuel receipts and shift operations. This follows §12's literal text ("Accountant | Cash variance approval" is the only role named for this area) rather than extending it to other plausible-sounding roles. Separately: approving a `ShiftReconciliationAmendment` recomputes the reconciliation's dependent figures but does NOT retroactively clear a prior `authorized_by`/`authorized_at` - that remains a true record of what was approved at the time, even if a later amendment changes the numbers. Whether it should instead force re-authorization is still open. | Owner | OPEN |
| 24 | §8 (adapted) | What "authorized" means for a credit sale | **Minimal (Phase D).** A credit-category `ShiftTender` requires only `shift_reconcile:record` permission (the same as any other tender) and a `credit_customer_id` reference - there is no credit-limit check, no per-customer approved-product list, and no separate higher-authority sign-off beyond holding that permission. Blueprint §8's "Approvers / restrictions" master-data concept is not built. | Owner | OPEN — deferred to Phase E |
| 25 | §6/§9 (interaction) | Whether exceptional litres (test draws / calibration) reduce expected sales revenue | **Not implemented - revenue is unaffected by exceptional events.** `app/core/revenue.py::compute_expected_fuel_revenue` prices a nozzle's full `dispensed_litres` (closing − opening totalizer); it does not subtract the litres a `ShiftExceptionalEvent` marks as test/calibration draws for that nozzle. Those litres are correctly excluded from the *stock* ledger's `SALE` entry (posted instead as `TEST_CALIBRATION`, see `shifts.py::approve_shift`), so book stock is accurate, but the *revenue* figure still prices them as if sold. Whether a calibration draw should reduce expected revenue to zero, and - if so - which price applies when it spans a mid-shift price change, and whether the exclusion is per-nozzle or (as stock posting currently is) per-tank, are accounting/operational policy decisions, not something to invent. | Owner + accountant | OPEN |
| 26 | §4/§8 (adapted) | What a supplier is actually owed for a delivery | **Implemented (Phase 6), recorded directly rather than derived.** `SupplierInvoice.amount` is entered by a human, not computed from `TankerReceipt.accepted_quantity * some price` - this system has never captured a fuel *purchase* price anywhere (`FuelPrice` is the station's selling price). A `SupplierInvoice` may optionally reference the `TankerReceipt` it relates to, but nothing enforces that the amount matches any formula. Whether purchase pricing should eventually be captured per-receipt (enabling an automatic invoice) is open. | Owner + accountant | OPEN |
| 27 | — (not in blueprint) | Expense category taxonomy | **Deliberately free text (Phase 6).** `Expense.category` is an unconstrained string - this system does not invent a fixed chart-of-accounts-style category list. An accountant may want a controlled vocabulary (e.g. for consistent reporting/export); that would be a follow-up `ExpenseCategory` master-data table, not a hardcoded enum. | Accountant | OPEN |
| 28 | — (not in blueprint) | Inventory valuation (cost of goods sold) | **Not implemented.** `InventoryMovement.amount` records the money value of an individual receipt (cost) or sale (revenue) when given, but nothing computes a weighted-average or FIFO unit cost, inventory valuation, or margin - the dashboard's inventory view shows quantity on hand and a per-movement amount only, never a derived "stock value" or "profit" figure. This is a real gap before relying on this for margin analysis, not a nice-to-have. | Owner + accountant | OPEN |
| 29 | §12 (Phase 6) | Who may approve a supplier payment / an expense | **This implementation's own reasonable default, not named in blueprint §12.** `supplier_ledger:approve` (money leaving the business) follows the same pattern as `shift_reconcile:approve` - Accountant and Owner only, not Station Manager. `expense:approve`, by contrast, goes to Station Manager, Accountant and Owner - a routine operational expense (e.g. a pump repair) is treated as a station-management decision, not a strictly financial-oversight one, unlike a cash-variance or supplier-payment approval. Whether expense approval should be narrowed to match the stricter pattern is open. | Owner | OPEN |
| 30 | — (production readiness, Phase 7) | Login brute-force protection scope | **Implemented as a single-instance, in-memory sliding-window limiter only** (`app/core/rate_limit.py`, 10 attempts / 5 minutes per email+IP, reset on success). This is real protection for the single-container deployment this system runs as today, but it is explicitly NOT sufficient once more than one backend replica runs behind a load balancer - each replica has its own counter, so an attacker spread across requests hitting different replicas is only partially throttled. A shared store (Redis `INCR`+TTL is the standard pattern) is required before horizontal scaling; see docs/REMAINING_RISKS.md. | Owner / whoever operates production | OPEN |
| 31 | — (production readiness, Phase 7) | Evidence/attachment storage durability | **Still local disk** (`app/core/storage.py`), unchanged from the Phase B assumption already on record. Phase 7 hardened what is validated before a file is written (real magic-byte content checking against the declared type, not just the client's Content-Type header; chunked reads with an early size-limit abort instead of buffering an unbounded body) but did not change WHERE files are stored. This remains a hard blocker for any deployment with more than one backend replica or any expectation that uploaded evidence survives a container being recreated - see docs/REMAINING_RISKS.md. | Owner | OPEN |
| 32 | — (production readiness, Phase 7) | Running `alembic upgrade head` automatically on every container start | **Unchanged from Phase A, now explicitly flagged as a multi-replica risk.** `docker-entrypoint.sh` running the migration on every boot is fine and convenient for the single-instance deployment this has run as through every phase. With more than one backend replica, a deploy that includes a migration can have multiple containers race to apply it concurrently, which Alembic does not guard against with a lock by default - the likely outcome is one or more replicas failing to start (not data corruption, since each migration runs inside its own transaction), not a silent problem. docs/DEPLOYMENT.md's production section describes the safer pattern (migrate as a separate one-off step before scaling up); this code was not changed to force that, since doing so would break the established single-instance dev/staging convenience documented in the README. | Owner / whoever operates production | OPEN |
| 33 | — (production readiness, Phase 7) | True duplicate-submission protection for pure-create endpoints | **Only state-transition endpoints are structurally duplicate-proof** (approve/close/reject/reverse - each re-checks status under a row lock, so a replay gets 409, extensively tested since Phase A). A plain create (`POST .../tenders`, `.../cash-movements`, `.../expenses`, `.../supplier-payments`, `.../credit-customers/{id}/payments`, `.../inventory-movements`) has no server-side idempotency key - two otherwise-identical requests (a double-click, a retried network request) create two separate rows. Phase 7 added a client-side submit-button guard to the one new UI form that creates money/stock movements (the inventory-movement form), which stops the most common real-world cause, but this is not a complete fix. The complete fix is an `Idempotency-Key` header + a server-side dedup table, not yet built - see docs/REMAINING_RISKS.md for why it wasn't built in this pass rather than half-built under time pressure. | Owner | OPEN |
| 34 | — (UI/UX upgrade) | One shift per business day | **The Daily Entry workspace's own simplifying convention, not a backend rule.** The backend has always supported many shifts a day per station (e.g. a morning and evening shift with different cashiers); the new "Daily Entry" workspace (`frontend/.../daily-entry/`) picks the single most-recently-opened shift for the selected business date and treats it as "today's entry" end to end. A station that genuinely runs multiple shifts per calendar day will see only one of them in this workspace - the underlying shift-by-shift API and the existing per-shift pages still work for that case. Whether to extend the workspace to list/switch between multiple shifts a day is open. | Owner | OPEN |
| 35 | — (UI/UX upgrade) | "Today" means the station's configured timezone, not the viewer's | **Implemented.** `app/api/shifts.py::list_shifts`'s `business_date` filter and the frontend's own "today" defaults (`lib/date.ts::stationDateIso`, used by the Daily Entry and Today's Overview pages) both resolve a business day using `Station.timezone` (an IANA zone, e.g. "Asia/Karachi"; defaults to "UTC" if never set). An invalid/unrecognized timezone string falls back to UTC on both the frontend and backend rather than erroring. The existing report endpoints (`app/api/reports.py`'s `date_from`/`date_to` range filters) were deliberately left UTC-based, not changed to be timezone-aware too - a report spanning a date *range* is far less likely to silently show "nothing" from an hours-wide boundary mismatch than a single-day shift lookup was (a real bug this phase found and fixed), and widening that scope wasn't needed to fix the actual defect. | Owner | OPEN — report ranges still UTC |
| 36 | — (UI/UX upgrade) | A new Daily Entry can only be started for today | **Implemented, deliberately restrictive.** `frontend/.../daily-entry/page.tsx` only shows "Start today's entry" when the selected business date equals the station's own "today" - picking a past empty date shows a message that a missing day is a correction, not a new entry, rather than a create button. This follows the same immutability-first posture as every other phase (approved records are corrected via amendment/reversal, never backdated) - the backend has no way to create a shift with a chosen `opened_at` at all, so this is the frontend staying consistent with that, not a new restriction invented on top of looser backend behavior. | Owner | OPEN — no backfill workflow exists yet if a day is genuinely missed |
| 37 | — (UI/UX upgrade) | Where a user's language preference (English / Roman Urdu) lives | **Client-only, per-device (`frontend/lib/i18n/context.tsx`), not per-account.** `User` has no `preferred_language` column, so switching language in the sidebar or Users & Settings only writes `localStorage` on that one browser/device - the same person signing in on a different device sees English again, and the choice is never visible to anyone else or to the backend. The fallback is deliberate (the UI spec that asked for this said to use a documented client preference when no backend field exists yet), not an oversight; adding a real per-account default means a migration adding the column plus a small `PATCH /auth/me` (or similar) to set it. | Owner | OPEN — client-only until a backend field is added |

## How to update this file

When the owner or accountant confirms a rule, change its `Status` to `CONFIRMED`, add the date and who confirmed it in a note, and — if the confirmed rule differs from the implemented default — open a follow-up change to the relevant model/config rather than leaving code and this document out of sync.
